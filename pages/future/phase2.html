FUTURE DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP

From Automatic Dispensing to Intelligent Water Management.

AquaSense Phase 1 establishes the core touchless dispensing platform using ultrasonic sensing, Arduino-based control, LCD feedback and servo actuation. Phase 2 proposes the next evolution: connectivity, monitoring, data collection, intelligent control and smarter management of water usage.

THE FOUNDATION

What Phase 1 Already Establishes

Phase 2 does not replace the existing prototype. It builds on the sensing, embedded control, mechanical actuation and enclosure development completed during Phase 1.

01

Touchless Detection

The HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor detects the presence of a user's hand within the programmed operating range.

02

Embedded Control

Arduino Uno processes the sensor measurement and executes the programmed dispensing sequence.

03

Mechanical Actuation

Dual SG90 servo motors mechanically operate the tap according to the controller commands.

04

Custom Mechanical Design

Dedicated 3D-designed enclosures organize and protect the electronic and servo components.

WHY EVOLVE?

Automation Solves Only Part of the Problem

An automatic tap can reduce unnecessary physical contact, but a truly intelligent water system should also understand how much water is being used, identify unusual usage patterns and provide useful information for better management.

?

How Much Water?

Phase 1 controls dispensing but does not directly measure the exact quantity of water flowing through the system.

!

Is There Abnormal Usage?

A standalone controller cannot easily provide remote alerts about unusual consumption or possible leakage.

↑

Can Usage Be Optimized?

Without historical data, it is difficult to identify trends, compare usage or make data-driven water-saving decisions.

PHASE 2 ROADMAP

Proposed Smart-System Upgrades

The following features represent a future development direction for AquaSense. They are proposed enhancements and are not presented as completed Phase 1 features.

01
CONNECTIVITY

ESP32-Based Controller

A future version could migrate from the Arduino Uno to an ESP32-based controller to provide integrated Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connectivity.

02
MEASUREMENT

Water Flow Monitoring

Adding a compatible flow sensor could allow the system to estimate actual water consumption rather than monitoring only dispensing events.

03
STORAGE

Water-Level Monitoring

A future tank-level sensing system could provide information about available water and generate low-level notifications.

04
IoT

Remote Dashboard

Wi-Fi connectivity could enable operating data to be transmitted to a web or mobile dashboard for remote monitoring.

05
DATA

Usage Analytics

Stored consumption data could be analyzed by hour, day or month to understand usage patterns and identify opportunities for conservation.

06
SAFETY

Intelligent Alerts

Future software could generate alerts for abnormal flow duration, unexpected water usage, low tank level or other predefined conditions.

DEVELOPMENT STRATEGY

A Practical Phase 2 Build Sequence

Future development should be incremental. Each upgrade should be tested independently before integrating the entire connected system.

01

Stabilize Phase 1

Confirm reliable hand detection, repeatable servo movement, stable power delivery and consistent mechanical operation.

02

Upgrade the Controller

Rebuild the embedded control layer around an ESP32 or another suitable connected microcontroller.

03

Add Measurement

Integrate and calibrate water-flow and level sensing before attempting advanced analytics.

04

Build Connectivity

Establish reliable communication between the embedded controller and a remote application or server.

05

Develop Dashboard

Create a clear interface for system status, water usage, history and important notifications.

06

Validate the Full System

Test reliability, measurement accuracy, network behavior, safety and long-term operation under realistic conditions.

LONG-TERM POTENTIAL

Where the Concept Could Expand

With further engineering and validation, the AquaSense concept could be adapted beyond a single prototype into different water-use environments.

H

Touchless dispensing combined with basic household water-use monitoring.

C

Campuses

P

I

Future multi-device deployments could provide centralized monitoring of multiple water-dispensing points.

ENGINEERING NOTE

Future Features Must Be Validated

Phase 2 is a development roadmap rather than a completed implementation. Adding IoT connectivity, flow measurement, remote dashboards and analytics introduces new engineering requirements including sensor calibration, electrical reliability, cybersecurity, data handling, waterproofing and long-term system testing.

✓

Validate Sensors

Flow and level sensors must be tested and calibrated before their data can be considered reliable.

✓

Protect Electronics

Any system operating near water requires careful enclosure, insulation, power and moisture protection design.

✓

Secure Connectivity

Connected versions should use appropriate authentication, communication security and responsible data handling.

THE VISION

Sense. Control. Measure. Understand.

AquaSense begins with a simple engineering objective: dispense water automatically without requiring direct physical contact. The long-term opportunity is broader. By combining sensing, embedded systems, mechanical automation, connectivity and data, the project can evolve into a platform that not only controls water delivery but also helps understand how water is being used.
